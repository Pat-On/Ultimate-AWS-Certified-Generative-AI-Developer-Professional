# 305. More Preparation Resources and the Exam Guide

The course covers everything the exam guide touches on, but extra review helps, especially practice exams.

## Extra resources: AWS Skill Builder

- **Free tier** is probably enough. It includes a **20-question official practice set** and text-based courses that review much of this course in a more concise format. Do the 20 questions.
- **Subscription tier** (about $30 a month) adds walkthroughs of sample questions (why each answer is or is not correct) and a **full-length practice exam**. Cancel it when you no longer need it. This course also has its own practice exam, so the standard plan may be enough.

## Why practice exams matter

- The professional-level exam is not "which service does X?". It gives complex scenarios and several complex candidate solutions.
- Budget **2 to 3 minutes per question** to understand the scenario and the options.
- It tests putting the pieces together, not recall. Practice exams (AWS's, this course's, anyone's) are the way to build that skill: the more, the better.

## The exam guide

Found via the exam's home page (where you also schedule it and see pricing), under "Prepare for exam". Pricing seen in the lecture was beta pricing and will differ.

- **Scope:** RAG systems and knowledge bases, integrating foundation models into applications and workflows, prompt engineering and management, agentic AI, optimising applications, security, governance and responsible AI, troubleshooting and monitoring, and model evaluation.
- **Target candidate:** at least 2 years building on AWS or with open-source technologies, plus AI/ML or data engineering experience and 1 year of hands-on AI solution work. AWS basics (compute, storage, networking, S3) are assumed.
- **Out of scope:** model development and training, advanced ML techniques (the machine learning specialty territory, which is being deprecated), and data and feature engineering (the Data Engineer Associate territory).
- **Scoring:** 750 out of 1000 to pass, pass/fail. **10 questions are unscored** trial questions, so an off-the-wall question is probably one of them.

Safety, security and governance ranks third, higher than is usual, so give it proper attention.

### How to use the guide

- Use the task and skill statements as a self-check: for each, ask "do I really know this?", and re-review where not.
- Check the appendix of in-scope topics and the in-scope services list. Everything in them should be covered in the course, apart from deprecated items. For example, AWS Chatbot has already been removed from the list (the transcript's wording of its replacement is garbled, probably Amazon Q Developer), so the list changes over time. Tell the instructor if something new appears.
- There is also a list of services you do not need to know.
